Seal custody simulator

What is this?

A smart seal travels with a vaccine shipment and records temperature, shocks and whether the box was opened. Each record is locked to the one before it, and a compact fingerprint of every batch is saved on a ledger shared by four organisations (manufacturer, transporter, pharmacy, regulator). At the destination, an independent check re-reads everything and decides ACCEPT, QUARANTINE or REJECT.

The point: if someone later edits the records to hide a problem, the change is detected. This simulator lets you play the honest shipment and the dishonest transporter.

Plain-English glossary
Hash chain
Each record includes the fingerprint of the previous one, so changing, deleting or reordering a record breaks the chain.
Merkle root
One fingerprint for a whole batch of records. Change any record and the root no longer matches.
Digital signature
Proof that a specific key (the seal's, or a party's) approved something. Forging one needs that key.
Ledger
A record book kept by four independent organisations. New entries need at least 3 of 4 to agree.
Custody handoff
Responsibility passes from one party to the next only when both the sender and the receiver sign.
Counter
A number that only goes up. It stops an old signed message from being replayed as new.
Off-chain records
The full raw readings, kept outside the ledger. Only their fingerprints go on it.

Limits used here (2–8°C, 15-minute excursion, 2.5 g shock) are demonstration values, not medical or regulatory thresholds. The system detects inconsistent evidence; it cannot prove a sensor was physically truthful.

Why this matters

Existing systems answer:
“Where is the vaccine stock and what is the recorded temperature?”

Our project additionally asks:
“Can multiple parties independently verify that the shipment history and custody record were not quietly altered after the event?”

Monitoring+Custody+Cryptographic evidence+Multi-party verification

A different trust requirement, not a claim of being better than existing systems. eVIN's loggers sit in storage equipment (UNDP); the seal is attached to the shipment and signs evidence at each handoff.

What changes: a transporter can no longer gain from hiding an excursion. As the run shows, an honest record can lead to inspection, while an edited one is exposed as edited. A realistic first step is a small pilot: seals on a few shipments along one state-store-to-district-store route, read alongside the existing records, measuring detection, time to verdict and cost per seal. Nothing is replaced.

What this does not prove

Important limitation
“A valid cryptographic signature proves that the data was produced by the registered device/key. It does not prove that a physical sensor could never have been fooled.”
  • Sensor spoofing
  • Physical sensor manipulation
  • Key-extraction attacks
  • Validator majority collusion
  • Loss of every off-chain copy
The full list is in “What this does not protect against” below.
How I would explain this to a judge
“India already has large-scale vaccine cold-chain monitoring. Our project addresses a different question: when multiple independent organizations handle the same shipment, how can they later verify that the shipment history was not quietly rewritten by one participant? The Smart Seal creates device-rooted evidence, while the permissioned shared ledger stores compact cryptographic proofs and custody events that multiple parties can independently verify.”

What this does not protect against

  • Physical sensor spoofing. A signature shows which device produced a reading, not that the reading is true. Redundant probes help only if both are not fooled.
  • Lab-grade key extraction from the secure element.
  • Majority validator collusion (3 of 4 here).
  • Jamming. The seal keeps logging offline, but anchoring is delayed.
  • Loss of every off-chain copy. The chain proves data existed but cannot restore it, so replicate across parties.
