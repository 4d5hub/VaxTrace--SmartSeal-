What is this?

A smart seal travels with a vaccine shipment and records temperature, shocks and whether the box was opened. Each record is locked to the one before it, and a compact fingerprint of every batch is saved on a ledger shared by four organisations (manufacturer, transporter, pharmacy, regulator). At the destination, an independent check re-reads everything and decides ACCEPT, QUARANTINE or REJECT.

The point: if someone later edits the records to hide a problem, the change is detected. This simulator lets you play the honest shipment and the dishonest transporter.

Plain-English glossary
Hash chain
Each record includes the fingerprint of the previous one, so changing, deleting or reordering a record breaks the chain.
Merkle root
One fingerprint for a whole batch of records. Change any record and the root no longer matches.
Digital signature
Proof that a specific key (the seal's, or a party's) approved something. Forging one needs that key.
Ledger
A record book kept by four independent organisations. New entries need at least 3 of 4 to agree.
Custody handoff
Responsibility passes from one party to the next only when both the sender and the receiver sign.
Counter
A number that only goes up. It stops an old signed message from being replayed as new.
Off-chain records
The full raw readings, kept outside the ledger. Only their fingerprints go on it.

Limits used here (2–8°C, 15-minute excursion, 2.5 g shock) are demonstration values, not medical or regulatory thresholds. The system detects inconsistent evidence; it cannot prove a sensor was physically truthful.
